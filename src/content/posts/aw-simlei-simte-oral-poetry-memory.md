## I. The Language of Longing: Aw Simlei

In the highlands of southern Manipur, oral poetry has served as the living record of the Simte people. Unlike prose narratives that chronicle events linearly, poetic verses—known broadly as 'La'—compress deep emotional states, communal memories of ancestral lands, and intimate observations of nature into tightly structured rhythmic couplets.

The verse presented below reflects the traditional voice of solitude, filial affection, and attachment to the ancestral hills:

> O gentle hills of home, how my spirit longs for you;
> Through rolling green valleys, my thoughts forever wander.

## II. Metrical Structure and Indigenous Couplets

Traditional Simte poetry relies on semantic parallelism, tone cadence, and internal assonance rather than end-rhyme schemes common in Western poetic traditions. In paired lines, the second line frequently expands or mirrors the metaphor introduced in the first, balancing physical geography with inner psychological resonance.

Through documenting these oral poems, our aim is to preserve the cadence of archaic Simte vocabulary that risks fading from everyday colloquial speech, ensuring that future generations can access both the linguistic precision and the emotional warmth of our ancestors' voices.
